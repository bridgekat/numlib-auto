# What is in this repository

A formal library of numerical analysis in two layers, generated by LLM agents under the process
described in [README.md](README.md). The layers are two Lake libraries.

`Numlib` is the **backbone**: general mathematics, named for its subject and stated at the weakest
hypotheses that carry the proof. `NumlibSurface` holds one directory per textbook, aligned to that
text section by section, where each declaration instantiates a backbone result at the book's own
hypotheses. A surface reads as an integration test of the backbone against a published account of
the subject; if a surface result does not specialize something, that is a demand on the backbone
rather than licence to do new mathematics on the surface.

The dependency runs one way only, and the library split is what enforces it: `NumlibSurface`
imports `Numlib`, and the reverse is not a convention but an impossibility.

So far there is one project: **iterative methods for linear systems**, and the **eigenvalue layer**
that grew out of it. The backbone develops the Krylov spine — projection and Petrov–Galerkin
methods, Arnoldi and Lanczos, CG, CR and the minimal-residual specification, the Givens/QR transport
layer, Chebyshev convergence bounds — on top of a normed-space layer (Neumann series, condition
numbers, spectral radius), a variational layer (sesquilinear forms, Lax–Milgram, Galerkin) and a
nonlinear layer (Banach fixed point, Newton–Kantorovich).

The eigenvalue layer stands on the same compression: Courant–Fischer and Weyl, Rayleigh–Ritz with
the residual and Ritz-vector bounds, the power method and subspace iteration, normal operators, and
the Kaniel–Paige–Saad convergence of the Lanczos Ritz values. It is stated in the vocabulary of the
angle between a vector and a subspace, which is defined here because Mathlib's `angle` is between
two vectors of a real space.

Two conventions shape it. **An iterate is specified by what it optimizes, not by the algorithm that
computes it**, so a theorem about "MINRES" is a theorem about any sequence satisfying the
minimal-residual specification, and every implementation inherits it. And **a spectral hypothesis
is a quadratic-form bound**, not a list of eigenvalues, which passes to compressions — so the
Chebyshev-type bounds hold in an arbitrary inner product space, with no functional calculus and no
finite-dimensionality.

Three surfaces stand over it:

| library | source | coverage |
|---|---|---|
| `NumlibSurface.SaadSparse` | Saad, *Iterative Methods for Sparse Linear Systems* (2nd ed., 2003) | §1.11–1.13, §4.1–4.2, Chapters 5 and 6 |
| `NumlibSurface.FongSaunders` | Fong and Saunders, *CG versus MINRES* (2012) | complete |
| `NumlibSurface.AtkinsonHan` | Atkinson and Han, *Theoretical Numerical Analysis* (3rd ed., 2009) | §2.3–2.5, §3.3–3.7, §5.1–5.4, §5.6, §8.2–8.3, §8.7, Chapter 9 |

Surface declarations are named for the results they state, so the name is the index: `theorem_6_29`
is Saad's Theorem 6.29 and `equation_6_43` is his (6.43). Each book's root module is its index — the
section-by-section outline, the ambient conventions, what is deferred, and the places where
formalizing the book corrected it. There are several of those: Atkinson–Han's (3.4.5) `‖P_K‖ = 1`
fails for `K = {0}`, and the two Table 5.1 counterexamples that Fong and Saunders assert without
witnesses are supplied here.

The library builds with no `sorry`, no warnings, and nothing beyond the three standard axioms. One
node of the plan is open: the gap form of the subspace-iteration bound, which wants a quantitative
continuity of an orthogonal projector in the family that spans it, and Mathlib has no such thing.

`tracker/` is the plan, in the TOML that [tracker](https://github.com/bridgekat/tracker) reads: a
copy of both source trees with `.lean` replaced by `.toml`, since a group is the plan for the module
its path names, and a node for each result the library stands behind. After `lake build`,
`tracker status` reports the counts, `tracker show` a group's brief or one node with its signature
and real dependencies, `tracker ready` the modules that can be worked on now, and `tracker graph`
the dependency graph. `tracker/README.md` states the conventions the plan is written by, and the
Markdown beside it carries the reasoning the TOML cannot hold — the design record of the backbone,
and each book's alignment to its surface.
